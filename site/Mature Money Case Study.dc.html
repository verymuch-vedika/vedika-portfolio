01

Quick read

Problem

Branch, telephony, app and web each verified customers differently. Someone verified in one channel often had to start again in another.

What I did

Rebuilt how ID&V actually worked, SME by SME, then ran channel-specific ideation before converging on one cross-channel concept.

Outcome

Three scored concepts, each with a 3-month and 3-year plan, handed to an accountable owner at the client and now rolling out.

02

The problem

At Mature Money, identification and verification (ID&V), the process that confirms who a customer is before they can transact, had grown fragmented across the bank’s channels.

Branch, telephony, app and web each handled ID&V differently, with no unified view of a customer’s verification status. A customer verified in one channel often had to re-verify from scratch in another. This severely increased frustration and made them feel mistrusted.

  • 1000+Customer complaints analysed
  • 4Channels, four different processes
  • 0Single source of truth

An early analysis of those complaints surfaced ID&V as a recurring frustration across every channel. The larger problem was that there was no shared vision of how ID&V should actually work.

What we had to solve

  1. 01

    Stop ID&V failing at the first check

    Customers didn’t know what they’d be asked to prove, so checks often failed on details they couldn’t recall or couldn’t access in the moment.

  2. 02

    Protect the customers most likely to be left behind

    Older and less digitally confident customers relied most on branches, where identification checks were the least consistent.

  3. 03

    Make telephony ID&V less costly to run

    Every unnecessary re-verification on the phone cost time, and time meant operational cost and dissatisfied customers.

04

Work and approach

  • 12 hrsInterviews with 9 SMEs
  • 3Top UK banks’ ID&V benchmarked
  • 9As-is blueprints in 3 months
  • 4Workshops: 3 by channel, 1 cross-channel

Step 01

Reconstructing the as-is, channel by channel

I mapped the branch journey and five telephony journeys myself. Each was built with SMEs and checked back with them before I treated it as ground truth.

Step 02

Running the room, not just contributing to it

I curated who needed to be in each session, shaped what each 1.5 to 2 hour session would cover, and facilitated every one of them.

Step 03

Keeping stakeholders inside the process, weekly

Weekly decks covering progress, blockers and next steps gave the client and leadership continuous visibility, rather than a single reveal at the end.

Ideation

Rather than one mixed-channel session, I ran three separate ones, so channel-specific friction could surface before being asked to converge into anything shared.

From that output, plus what I knew was already in flight elsewhere, I synthesised a single unifying concept.

The unifying concept

One ID&V identity, usable across products and channels, with step-up verification only when a customer’s risk tier actually changes.

A follow-up session turned this into three concepts and we scored each one on impact and effort. Then I checked those scores with SMEs in branch, telephony and digital, against the problems they saw every day.

05

The solution

37Ideas generated
3Concepts, scored on impact and effort
3Quick wins
6Long-term solutions on a roadmap
  1. 01

    Awareness of ID&V questions and documents

    Customers and colleagues often didn’t know in advance what a verification step would ask for. Surfacing it upfront reduces failed attempts and repeat contact.

  2. 02

    One customer ID&V across the bank

    One identity across products and channels. A customer verified at Tier 2 doesn’t start from zero when a Tier 3 product asks for more; they only add what the higher tier requires.

  3. 03

    Consistent language across channels and colleagues

    The same question, asked the same way on the phone, in branch or in the app, removing a subtle but real source of confusion and mistrust.

Reaching past scope

Any real unification concept had to account for both merging entities, not just Mature Money’s side. So I reached out to Countrywide Building Society (CBS’s) design team. It wasn’t formally scoped, but it was necessary if the concept was going to hold up.

They couldn’t answer. Key people were on leave, and CBS’s ID&V process wasn’t documented in enough detail for their own team to explain how it would integrate after the merger.

06

Where it stands

Rolling outConcepts and roadmap shipped, gradually being rolled out

The concepts and roadmap were shipped to the client, and the first changes were already rolling out when I moved to another engagement. As a service design engagement, the goal was to surface the right solutions and hand them to someone accountable for building them.

07

Learnings

  • Let the people closest to the problem check the scores

    Scoring concepts as a team is fast, but taking the scores back to branch, telephony and digital SMEs showed which ideas would actually ease their day.

  • Set up sessions so every voice is heard

    A separate session for each channel meant every team’s problems got heard, instead of the loudest voice taking over a mixed room.

Smaller things I’m taking with me

Make the plan visible from day one

A shared plan from the start made scope conversations quicker and kept everyone pointed the same way.

Document the work as it happens

A weekly record of decisions kept progress visible to the client and my own thinking sharp.

Design for today, leave room for change

With a merger underway, I fixed how customers are verified now and kept the concepts flexible enough to fit whatever came next.
